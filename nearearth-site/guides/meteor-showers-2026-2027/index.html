Asteroids & meteors guide

Meteor Shower Calendar 2026–2027: Dates, Rates and Moon Phases

Meteor showers happen when Earth passes through the dust left behind by a comet or asteroid. The same showers return on the same dates every year, but the Moon changes, and it makes the biggest difference to how many you'll see.

The calendar

Peak dates are the night most worth watching; most showers are active for a few nights either side. The rate is the ZHR, the number an observer would see per hour under a perfectly dark sky with the radiant overhead. In practice expect a third to half of that.

The best ones to plan for

  • Geminids, 13–14 December 2026. The strongest shower of the year, with a thin crescent Moon that sets in the early evening. Meteors start from about 9 pm and continue all night.
  • Quadrantids, 3–4 January 2027. A short, sharp peak lasting only a few hours, but rich when you catch it. Best from northern Europe and North America in the hours before dawn.
  • Eta Aquariids, 6 May 2027. Debris from Halley's Comet, with a new Moon. Best from the southern hemisphere and the tropics, in the last hours before dawn.

How to watch

  • Get away from street lights and give your eyes 20 minutes to adjust. Don't look at your phone, or use a red screen.
  • Lie back and look at a wide area of sky about halfway up. Meteors appear anywhere, not just near the radiant.
  • After midnight is usually better: your side of Earth then faces the direction Earth is travelling, so it meets more meteors.
  • Dress warmly, even in summer. You'll be still for a long time.

Good to know

Quick answers

When is the next big meteor shower?

The Geminids, peaking on the night of 13–14 December 2026, with up to about 150 meteors an hour under dark skies and little moonlight.

What time is best to watch a meteor shower?

Usually after midnight and before dawn, when the radiant is high. The Geminids are an exception and are good from mid-evening.

Do I need a telescope?

No. Meteors move too fast and across too much sky for a telescope or binoculars. Your eyes are the best tool.
