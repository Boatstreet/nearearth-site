The next big one is the total solar eclipse of 2 August 2027, with over six minutes of darkness near Luxor and a deep partial eclipse across most of Europe. Here is every eclipse until 2030 and what it will look like from major cities.

Lunar eclipses

A lunar eclipse is visible from anywhere the Moon is up at the time, so they are much easier to catch than solar eclipses. A total lunar eclipse turns the Moon a deep red.

Watching a solar eclipse safely

Never look at the Sun without certified eclipse glasses (ISO 12312-2) or a proper solar filter, even when most of it is covered. Sunglasses are not safe. You can also project the Sun through a pinhole onto card. Only during the few minutes of totality, when the Sun is completely covered, is it safe to look directly.

Good to know

Quick answers

When is the next total solar eclipse in Europe?

On 2 August 2027, totality crosses the far south of Spain near Cádiz and Gibraltar. Most of Europe sees a partial eclipse that morning.

How much of the eclipse will London see in 2027?

About 42% of the Sun will be covered from London at around 09:00 UTC (10:00 BST) on 2 August 2027.

When is the next total lunar eclipse visible from the UK?

On 31 December 2028, followed by a long one on 26 June 2029.
